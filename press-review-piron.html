Batavia Community Moves to Permanently Endow Scholarship Honoring INCubator Teacher Dennis Piron

Dunham Foundation will match the community's first $50,000 dollar-for-dollar, creating a $100,000 endowment that funds a graduating senior every year — permanently

BATAVIA, Ill. — A group of Batavia business leaders and former mentors has launched a campaign to permanently endow a scholarship honoring Dennis Piron, the Batavia High School teacher who has spent a decade turning students into entrepreneurs through the school's INCubator program.

The Dennis Piron Legacy Scholarship will be awarded annually to a graduating Batavia High School senior. Because the fund is endowed rather than raised year to year, the award continues indefinitely without further fundraising.

The campaign has a $100,000 goal, structured as two halves. The Dunham Foundation has approved a $50,000 dollar-for-dollar matching grant, released once the community raises and delivers the first $50,000. More than $36,000 has already been committed by an initial group of lead donors.

The scholarship is held and administered by the Batavia Foundation for Educational Excellence, an IRS-registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit.

“Every one of us on this committee watched Dennis do something for kids that most schools never manage. We didn't want that to end when he does. Endowing it means a Batavia student gets that push every single year, forever.” — Rob Hollis, Piron Legacy Scholarship Committee

A decade of building entrepreneurs

Through the Batavia High School INCubator program, Piron has guided students as they turn raw ideas into real businesses — coaching them through everything from pro-forma financials to Pitch Night, where teams present to a room full of investors.

The program's results are visible around town. This spring, a Windmill Weekly team of Batavia High students built a large-type newspaper for residents of care centers and assisted-living facilities, took the top donation prize at INCubator's Pitch Night, and held a Batavia Chamber of Commerce ribbon-cutting in July. The idea began with a student's grandmother, who missed her newspaper while living in a care center.

How to give

Donations can be made at pironlegacy.org, where the campaign's progress is posted publicly. Gifts are tax-deductible to the extent allowed by law. Because the Dunham match is released only after the community's $50,000 is received, fulfilled gifts — not pledges alone — are what unlock the matching funds.

The effort is organized by a volunteer steering committee of Batavia business leaders and former INCubator mentors: Dave Brown, Bob Hansen, Rob Hollis, Bob Hubbard, Gary Ricke and Bob Vaughan.
