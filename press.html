The Dennis Piron Legacy Scholarship will be awarded annually to a graduating Batavia High School senior. Because the fund is endowed rather than raised year to year, the award continues indefinitely with no further fundraising.

The Dunham Foundation has approved a $50,000 dollar-for-dollar matching grant. The grant is released once the community's first $50,000 has been raised and received — so fulfilled gifts, not pledges alone, are what unlock the match.

The scholarship is held and administered by the Batavia Foundation for Educational Excellence, an IRS-registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Contributions are tax-deductible to the extent allowed by law.

The effort is organized by a volunteer steering committee of Batavia business leaders and former INCubator mentors: Dave Brown, Bob Hansen, Rob Hollis, Bob Hubbard, Gary Ricke and Bob Vaughan.

Background

Suggested background copy — free to quote or adapt.

Through the Batavia High School INCubator program, Dennis Piron has guided students as they turn raw ideas into real businesses — coaching them through everything from pro-forma financials to Pitch Night, where student teams present to a room full of investors.

Piron is retiring from teaching. He remains head football coach through the coming season, and the INCubator program passes to his daughter, Alex Piron, who takes over as its new lead.

The program's results are visible around Batavia. In 2026, a student team built The Windmill Weekly, a large-type newspaper for residents of care centers and assisted-living facilities, took the top donation prize at INCubator's Pitch Night, and held a Batavia Chamber of Commerce ribbon-cutting in July. The idea began with a student's grandmother, who missed her newspaper while living in a care center.

The scholarship exists to make that kind of mentorship permanent — rewarding, every year, a graduating senior who carries the same spirit forward.
