Why This Scholarship

A decade of building entrepreneurs

Through the Batavia High School INCubator program, Dennis Piron guided countless students as they turned raw ideas into real businesses — coaching them through everything from pro-forma financials to pitch night in front of a room full of investors.

He taught them courage, teamwork, and what it feels like to bring something to life. This scholarship — awarded annually to a graduating senior who carries that spirit forward — makes his impact permanent.

"There's a tough love that prepares them in ways I don't know that college does. Nobody like him. Nobody." — Gary Ricke, Mentor

In the News

This is what his mentorship builds

Daily Herald · July 17, 2026

Getting the word out

Student-run Batavia newspaper for seniors earns top prize at pitch night, plans to expand

When Batavia High School senior Bryan Wnek's grandmother was living in a care center, what she missed most was her newspaper. In Dennis Piron's INCubator class, that observation became a company. Guided by Piron, Wnek and classmates Ben Manikas and Lexie Kammes built The Windmill Weekly — a 12- to 16-page tabloid, printed in oversized type, for residents of care centers and assisted-living facilities.

At the INCubator's Pitch Night, their pitch won the program's top donation prize: a $3,400 check for equipment, a website, and marketing. The Daily Herald caught up with the team this month as they prepare for a July 28 ribbon-cutting at the Batavia Chamber of Commerce and plan an expansion into Aurora, Geneva, and St. Charles. All three students are carrying the business into next school year.

A student's observation, coached into a business that serves its community — that is the work this scholarship exists to carry forward.
